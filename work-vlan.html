Network Segmentation Across a Three-Office Compound

Three separate office locations inside one large compound, all shouting on a single flat network. Broadcasts everywhere, stability nowhere.

The situation

A large compound with three physical office locations. Each location is self-contained: main office, staff accommodation, warehouse, and a working factory with its machinery — plus Wi-Fi access points, cameras, and printers. All of it sat on one flat network. Broadcast traffic and congestion made the whole thing unstable: a problem in one location was everybody's problem.

My job: split the compound into isolated segments so each location stands on its own — without breaking the locations still running on the old network during the rollout. Lab tested first, because the compound does not forgive guesswork.

Network Topology

[FortiGate NGFW — routing + firewall policies]
    |-- Location 1 (VLAN 20) -- [L3 Switch] -- office PCs, APs, cameras, printers, machinery
    |-- Location 2 (VLAN 30) -- [L3 Switch] -- office PCs, APs, cameras, printers, machinery
    +-- Location 3 (VLAN 40) -- [L3 Switch] -- office PCs, APs, cameras, printers, machinery
(each location: dedicated switch ports + segregated cabling, fully isolated)

Segments are fully isolated — no traffic between VLANs. Each location is its own broadcast and collision domain, enforced by FortiGate policies and per-location L3 switching.

Hardware Used

  • FortiGate Next-Generation Firewall (routing between segments + security policies)
  • 3× L3 switches — one main switch per location (VLANs, dedicated ports)
  • Segregated physical cabling per location + re-labelled patch panels
  • Wireless access points per location, printers and machinery on static IPs

What I actually did

Lab first (2 days): designed the segmentation, built it on a lab setup, planned the cutover order, and tested routing, policies, and failover behavior before touching anything live. Location 1: deployed VLAN 20 — configured the L3 switch, FortiGate routing and policies, access points, and static IPs for printers and machinery — then watched it for a full day for faults and load issues while Locations 2 and 3 stayed on the previous network, unaffected. Location 2: same cutover to VLAN 30, another day of watching for errors and load. Location 3: same cutover to VLAN 40. After the last watch period with zero issues, all three locations were on segmented, isolated networks.

Results

  • Broadcast congestion gone — each location is its own quiet domain.
  • A fault in one location stays in that location instead of taking down the compound.
  • Stable, secure, and fast: granular traffic control plus no cross-segment lateral movement.
  • Documentation: one-page VLAN + IP sheet per location. Future-me says thanks.
